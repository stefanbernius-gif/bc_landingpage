Questions, answered

Questions buyers and sellers ask

Why should my broker be an AMPI member in San Miguel de Allende?

AMPI is Mexico's leading professional association for real estate. Every AMPI San Miguel firm holds the real estate license the State of Guanajuato requires, and members share listings in real time through the MLS — so an AMPI listing reaches every member office in the city, not just one. BMB is led by a former President of AMPI San Miguel and the director of its Statistics Committee.

What are the stages of selling a property with BMB?

Three stages: MAPE (market analysis and property evaluation — documents, valuation and a full strategy report), MAP (the marketing and advertising package — MLS integration, professional photo and video, 3D tours, drone aerials, targeted campaigns) and CFU (closing and follow-up — notary procedures, optional third-party inspection and support after the sale).

How are BMB's commissions and fees set?

Commissions depend on the scope of services and are agreed openly with each client. BMB separates real estate commissions, tied to the transaction, from consulting fees, charged for expert advice.

Can I buy or sell in San Miguel de Allende if I don't speak Spanish?

Yes. BMB works in English and Spanish, guides international buyers through every step of the Mexican property process, and handles notary procedures and paperwork on the client's behalf.

Can foreigners own property in San Miguel de Allende?

Yes. San Miguel de Allende lies outside Mexico's restricted zone along the borders and coasts, so foreign buyers can generally hold title directly in their own name, without a bank trust (fideicomiso). As part of the purchase, the buyer signs an agreement with Mexico's Ministry of Foreign Affairs (SRE), known as the Calvo clause. The notary confirms the exact requirements for each purchase.

What does the notario público do in a property sale?

Every property sale in Mexico is formalized as a public deed before a notario público, a lawyer appointed by the state. The notary checks the title and any liens, calculates and withholds the taxes that apply to the sale, and sends the deed to the Public Registry of Property.

Can I renovate a house in the historic centre?

Usually yes, with permits. Since 2008, the Protective Town of San Miguel and the Sanctuary of Atotonilco have been a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the historic centre is protected. Before buying a house you plan to change, check with the municipality and INAH which approvals the work needs.
